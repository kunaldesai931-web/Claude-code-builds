# Deliverable 2 — The Framework

**A reusable 10-dimension screening template for international short-term-rental (STR) real estate, built for an Indian tax resident, and applied to Bali, Phuket, Gold Coast and Dubai.**

> This is the property analogue to an equity framework stack. The governing instinct is the one carried over from equities: **treat a headline rental yield with exactly the skepticism you would treat a stock's headline P/E.** A yield quoted before management fees, before the source-country tax, before the Indian-resident tax overlay, and before FX is not a thesis — it is marketing. The framework's job is to expose the friction layers that no source-country property pitch will show you.

**Source tiers:** T1 = regulatory/primary; T2 = institutional/professional; T3 = marketing/blog (directional only). `STALE` = >12 months old at May 2026. Single-sourced claims flagged. Where a government primary page blocked automated retrieval (several did: incometaxindia.gov.in, BPS, GlobalPropertyGuide, MEA treaty PDFs all returned 403), the claim is carried via T2 sources that reproduce the primary text and is flagged for direct verification before capital is committed.

---

## How to use this document

Each of the 10 dimensions below is a **screen**. A market or a specific property must pass each screen on its own terms; a strong score on one dimension does not buy forgiveness on another. The dimensions are ordered roughly by how often they *kill* a deal for an Indian resident, not by how they appear in a brochure — which is why **Tax (Dimension 5)** and **Legal/Structural (Dimension 1)** are weighted hardest, and why **Yield (Dimension 2)**, the investor's dominant criterion, is screened *only on a net-of-everything basis*.

## Dimension 1 — Legal & Structural

**The screen:** *Do you actually own a durable, transferable, enforceable interest — or a contract that decays, a quota you don't control, or a structure that is one regulatory change from illegal?*

| | What you actually get | Terminal-value behaviour | Killer risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bali** | No freehold. PT PMA + **HGB** (80-yr regime, 30+20+30) is the only sound path; leasehold (Hak Sewa, 25–30 yr) is a contract that **reverts to the landowner at expiry** | Leasehold → **wasting asset**, renewal renegotiated at then-market; HGB durable | Nominee structures **criminalised** (Perda 4/2026, up to 5 yr prison) [T2/T3, verify]; no title insurance |
| **Phuket** | Condo **freehold** within the 49% foreign quota (durable, but resale needs future quota headroom); villa **leasehold capped at 30 yrs** by 2025 Supreme Court ruling | Condo freehold durable; leasehold **30-yr hard stop** — auto-renewals void | Supreme Court 4655/2566 (18-Mar-2025) killed "30+30+30"; Thai nominee co. prosecuted with AI (820+ cases) [T1/T2] |
| **Gold Coast** | **Torrens freehold** — strongest, state-guaranteed indefeasible title of the four | Durable, appreciating | FIRB exemption survives **only as joint tenants** with citizen spouse; established-dwelling ban to 2029 if structure breaks [T1] |
| **Dubai** | **Freehold** in 40+ designated zones, DLD-registered; Golden Visa at AED 2M | Durable | Centralised DLD registry (no independent torrens-style indemnity); off-plan via Oqood carries completion risk |

**Verdict:** Gold Coast and Dubai give you a real, durable, freehold-equivalent interest. Bali and Phuket give you either a wasting contract (leasehold) or a freehold with a liquidity string attached (quota), and both have **criminalised the cheap workaround** — which tells you the regulatory direction of travel. *On Dimension 1, the high-headline-yield markets are the weak ones.*

**Inheritance/succession overlay (all markets):** India has no estate duty, and inheriting foreign property is not itself taxed in India [T2]. But the **foreign jurisdiction's law governs** — forced heirship, probate, or local estate tax can override an Indian will, and under FEMA 7(R) a **resident heir may only hold inherited foreign property if the deceased acquired it validly** (while non-resident, or via LRS/RFC). The heir's Indian residential status then determines ongoing Schedule FA and tax exposure. Write a **separate will per jurisdiction** and confirm the chain of FEMA-valid acquisition.

## Dimension 2 — Yield Economics (net of everything, or it is not a number)

**The screen:** *Strip the headline gross yield down through management, the full opex stack, source-country tax, and the Indian tax overlay. What is left is the only yield that exists.*

The decomposition, applied identically to every market:

```
Gross yield (headline, what the brochure shows)
  − Management fee (15–35% of revenue, market-specific)
  − Opex stack (strata/HOA/service charge, utilities, cleaning,
    platform commission, marketing, insurance, periodic refurbishment)
  = Net operating yield
  − Source-country tax on rental income
  = Net post-source-tax yield
  − Indian tax top-up (slab on net rent, less FTC for source tax paid)
  = NET POST-TAX INR YIELD  ← the only number that counts
```

| | Headline gross | Mgmt fee | Net operating yield (realistic) | Source tax on rent | India drag | **Net post-tax INR yield** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bali** | 10–15% (T3, optimistic) | 25–30% | ~6–7% | 20% gross WHT (credited) | top-up to ~31% | **~4.0–4.5%** |
| **Phuket** | 7–10% (T3) | 20–30% | ~4–6% | 15% WHT, not final; progressive 5–35% | top-up | **~3.0–4.0%** |
| **Gold Coast** | 4–5% | 15–25% | ~3% (high strata) | 30% from $1 (credited) | top-up | **~2.0–2.5%** |
| **Dubai** | 8–12% (T3) → 5–6% real | 15–25% | ~5–6% | **0% (nothing to credit)** | **full ~31%** | **~3.5–4.0%** |

**Key reads:**
- **Bali has the highest genuine net yield** — but it is attached to the weakest ownership (Dimension 1) and the least reliable FX (Dimension 7), and "9–12% net" marketing is upper-bound and transactionally biased.
- **Dubai's STR premium is largely illusory after the cost stack** — service charges (AED 10–30+/sqft), chiller, DET fees and summer voids pull the advertised 8–12% down toward long-let yields. And because there is **no source tax to credit, the full ~31% Indian drag bites** — the "tax-free" headline does nothing for you.
- **Gold Coast is the lowest-yielding** and is taxed at 30% from the first dollar with no threshold — it is an *appreciation/FX/optionality* play, not a yield play, and should not be bought for yield.
- **Refurbishment cycle:** STR furnishings turn every 5–7 years (3–5 in tropical Bali/Phuket); budget a capex reserve — per-sqm figures were not reliably sourced in any market [data gap].

**Anti-pattern rejected:** no figure in the "headline gross" column is a thesis. Only the right-hand column is.

## Dimension 3 — Appreciation Drivers

**The screen:** *Separate structural demand (infrastructure, durable tourism, migration) from cyclical froth (off-plan speculation, hot-money buyers). Strip FX out to see true local-asset appreciation, then add FX back to see the INR outcome.*

- **Bali:** Tourism is genuinely structural — 2025 foreign arrivals ~6.95M (+10%), India now the #2 source (~569k) behind Australia (~1.63M) [T1 BPS]. Infrastructure upside (North Bali airport, Gilimanuk–Mengwi toll) is **real but repeatedly slipped** — treat as optional, not base case. Offsetting: **villa supply +35% in 3 years**, concentrated oversupply in Canggu. *Demand structural, supply late-cycle.*
- **Phuket:** Arrivals 8.65M in 2024 (7% CAGR 2006–2024), India fastest-growing source (+58%). But **Thailand's 2025 total arrivals fell 7.2%** on China weakness, and **10,000+ condo units hit in 2026** atop ~7,000 unsold. Prime/well-located appreciates; commodity mid-market does not. *Sub-market selection is everything.*
- **Gold Coast:** The strongest *structural* appreciation case — QLD +~98k people in 2024–25 (79% from migration), Gold Coast +68k over 5 years, **2032 Brisbane Olympics** + Light Rail Stage 3, record A$8.1bn visitor spend; cumulative +73% since the pandemic. But affordability is stretched (~A$1.17m house median) and the rate tailwind reversed (below). *Best demand fundamentals, fully-priced entry.*
- **Dubai:** +78% from the 2020 trough, +18% YoY into Jan-2026 — but this is the cyclical signal, not a structural one. India is the **#1 source market (2.5M in 2024)**. The structural story (population growth, business migration) is real but is colliding with the largest supply pipeline of the four. *Demand real, valuation extended, supply heavy.*

**Sub-market selection criteria (the within-market screen):**
- Bali: Seminyak (mature/saturated) · Canggu (hot but oversupplied) · Uluwatu (emerging luxury, volatile demand) · Ubud (low ROI, retreat niche). *Prefer differentiated, zoned-compliant, scarcity-protected.*
- Phuket: Bang Tao/Laguna (branded, licensed) · Surin/Kamala (capital-growth) · Patong (yield, lower quality) · Rawai/Kata (mid). *Buy only hotel-licensed buildings for daily STR.*
- Gold Coast: Burleigh/Broadbeach (tightest vacancy 0.7%) · Main Beach/Surfers (STR revenue but high strata) · Palm Beach (emerging). 
- Dubai: avoid the 2026–27 supply-spike zones (JVC, Business Bay) for entry timing; Marina/Downtown/Palm for durability at lower yield.

**FX-stripped truth:** much of the "appreciation" Indian investors quote is INR depreciation, not asset growth. Always compute local-currency CAGR first, then add the INR conversion separately (Dimension 7).

## Dimension 4 — Cycle Positioning (Howard Marks + the 18-year land cycle)

**The screen:** *Where is each market on the pendulum — sentiment, supply, leverage, foreign-buyer enthusiasm — and where is the global land cycle?*

The macro layer is unambiguous and it is the most important single finding in this framework: **all four markets sit in the late-upswing window of the 18-year land cycle, which Patel/Anderson/Harrison place at a peak around 2026, ahead of a ~4-year downturn** [T2 — *The Secret Wealth Advantage* 2023; MoneyWeek 2021]. AirDNA's own 2026 outlook (16-Dec-2025) calls supply normalising and **RevPAR thin (~+0.5%)**, with **Asia-Pacific STR in the oversupply quadrant** [T1].

| | Sentiment | Supply | Leverage | Foreign-buyer enthusiasm | Marks read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bali** | Hot | **Over-extended** (Canggu) | Low (cash) | Very high | **Late, supply-driven** |
| **Phuket** | Warm | Rising (mid-mkt glut) | Low (cash) | High (Russia/India/China) | **Mid-to-late** |
| **Gold Coast** | Firm | Constrained | **High (mortgaged, RBA-driven)** | Moderate | **Mid, rate-sensitive** |
| **Dubai** | **Euphoric** | **Huge pipeline** | Moderate, growing | **Extreme** | **Late, momentum/euphoria** |

**Implication for deployment:** the framework's single most valuable instruction is **do not deploy the full envelope into a forecast peak.** Phase across the 3–5 year window deliberately to straddle the downturn; reserve the largest tranche for the cycle low (Marks: "buy when others are forced to sell"). Cash-buyer markets (Bali, Phuket, Dubai) survive a downturn better than the leveraged Gold Coast market in *price* terms — but Gold Coast's title/legal quality means a downturn there is a *price* event, not a *tenure* event, which is the safer kind to ride through.

**Late-cycle red flags actually present (not hypothetical):** Dubai off-plan priced above ready stock (63% of sales); Phuket developer guaranteed-yield schemes (yield baked into inflated price); Bali "12–20% ROI" off-plan marketing; foreign-buyer concentration (Russian) in both Phuket and Dubai.

## Dimension 5 — The Tax Stack (Indian-resident lens — the dominant dimension)

**The screen:** *Compute all-in tax leakage as a % of gross rental yield, and separately as a % of capital gain on exit. If this is misstated, nothing else in the analysis matters.*

### 5a. Acquisition costs
| | Headline acquisition tax | Foreign-buyer surcharge |
|---|---|---|
| Bali | BPHTB 5% + notary ~1% + PNBP | — (but PT PMA setup cost) |
| Phuket | Transfer 2% (often split) + SBT 3.3% or stamp 0.5% + lease reg 1.1% | — |
| Gold Coast | QLD transfer duty + **AFAD 8%** on foreign owner's interest [T1 QRO] | 8% (proportional to foreign share) |
| Dubai | **DLD 4%** (the only material acquisition cost) | — |

### 5b. The DTAA mechanism (the same logic in all four)
All four India–[country] treaties follow the OECD pattern the research confirmed:
- **Article 6 (Income from Immovable Property):** rental income "may be taxed" in the state where the property is situated — *non-exclusive*, so the source country taxes first.
- **Article 13 (Capital Gains):** gains on alienation of immovable property "may be taxed" in the situs state.
- **Elimination of double tax (Art. ~23/24/25):** India, as country of residence, taxes its resident on **worldwide income** and grants a **foreign tax credit** for source-country tax actually paid — **capped at the lower of (foreign tax paid) or (Indian tax on that income)** [T1 — Rule 128; treaty texts via T2, verify article numbers against primary PDFs].

The consequence everyone misses: **DTAA relief is by credit, not exemption.** You always end up paying **the higher of the two regimes**, and you can never be better off than the Indian rate.

### 5c. Where the foreign tax credit structurally fails (the four traps)
1. **Source rate < Indian rate → you top up to the Indian rate** (the credit caps out at the foreign tax).
2. **Zero-source-tax (the "Dubai illusion"):** UAE taxes nothing → nothing to credit → you pay the **full Indian tax**. "Tax-free Dubai" is tax-free only for non-residents [T2].
3. **Gross-vs-net mismatch:** where the source country withholds on **gross** rent (Bali 20%, Phuket 15%) but India taxes **net** (after the 30% standard deduction u/s 24, interest, municipal tax), the foreign tax can **exceed** the Indian tax on the smaller net base — and the **excess foreign tax is permanently lost** (no carry-forward in India).
4. **Form 67 omission:** FTC is **denied entirely** if Form 67 is not filed by the end of the assessment year — a fatal, common clerical error [T1].

### 5d. Capital gains on exit (the Indian side)
- LTCG if held >24 months; **12.5% flat, no indexation** post-23-Jul-2024 [T1 — CBDT FAQ PRID 2036604].
- **No foreign-currency protection for residents** — the s.48 forex proviso is NRI-only, so the gain is computed in **rupees** and **INR depreciation is taxed as if it were real gain** [T2]. This is the single most under-appreciated exit cost.
- **Surcharge cliff** — see worked example below.

### 5e. Worked example — the surcharge cliff (₹48L base income + ₹40L foreign LTCG, new regime, FY25-26)

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Base income | ₹48,00,000 |
| Foreign-property LTCG | ₹40,00,000 |
| **Total income** | **₹88,00,000 → crosses ₹50L → 10% surcharge band** |
| LTCG tax: 12.5% × 40L = ₹5.00L; +10% surcharge (within the 15% cap) | ₹5,50,000 |
| Base tax (new-regime slabs on 48L) ≈ ₹11.40L; **+10% surcharge** ₹1.14L | ₹12,54,000 |
| **+4% cess** | ~₹72,160 |
| **Total** | **≈ ₹18.76 lakh** |

**The cliff:** because the LTCG dragged total income over ₹50L, the **entire base income now attracts 10% surcharge (~₹1.14L) that would not exist** if total income had stayed ≤₹50L. The 15% cap protects the *gain* slice; it does **not** protect your salary/rent. **Mitigation: stagger realisations across financial years** (sell partial interests, sequence the 2–3 properties' exits, time sales to low-base-income years).

### 5f. All-in tax leakage (the headline numbers for an Indian resident)

| | Leakage as % of **gross rent** | Leakage as % of **capital gain on exit** | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bali** | ~30–35% (20% WHT + India top-up, some FTC leakage on gross basis) | ~13–15% India + 2.5% of gross sale price (Indonesia) | 2.5% is on **sale price not gain** — punishing on low-gain exits |
| **Phuket** | ~30–35% | ~13–15% (progressive Thai income tax credited against Indian 12.5%, top-up) | situs CGT via withholding |
| **Gold Coast** | ~30% (taxed at 30% from $1, credited) | **~30%+ effective** (no 50% discount + 15% FRCGW gross withholding) — **worst of the four** | exit tax is the dealbreaker |
| **Dubai** | **~31% (full Indian, zero credit)** | **12.5% (full Indian, zero credit)** | low exit rate is its one genuine tax advantage |

**Repatriation & reporting friction (all markets):** LRS USD 250k/yr cap → multi-year staging; 20% TCS on >₹10L remittances (refundable, but ~12–18 month cash lock); FX spreads 0.5–2%/leg; **Schedule FA every year regardless of value**; Black Money Act ₹10L/yr penalty for non-disclosure. There is **no inbound cap** on a resident repatriating their own LRS-funded asset proceeds (a common advisor error conflates this with the NRI USD-1M rule) [T1].

## Dimension 6 — STR Operating Model

**The screen:** *Is the rental model even legal, who runs it, what does it really cost, and how concentrated is the platform/regulatory risk?*

**Regulatory STR risk — current state and tail risk (the make-or-break sub-screen):**
- **Bali:** foreigners cannot hold a Pondok Wisata licence; compliant path is PT PMA + KBLI 55193 villa licence + tourism-zone ("pink") zoning + the **Mar-2026 NIB/OTA-licensing deadline**, enforced by demolitions/deportations. Tail risk = **compliance-driven delisting/sealing**, not (currently) a blanket ban [T2/T3].
- **Phuket:** **daily STR is illegal without a hotel licence** (Hotel Act, 30-day minimum rule); only purpose-built hotel-licensed condos qualify. Tail risk = a juristic-person rule change forcing 30-day minimums — **the single highest operating risk in the four markets** [T2].
- **Gold Coast:** **friendliest of the four** — City of Gold Coast light-touch registration, and crucially **QLD bodies corporate cannot ban STR** (BCCM Act s.180(4); *Hilton Park* QCAT). Tail risk = the **NSW template** (180-day cap + strata ban power) if housing politics tighten [T1/T2].
- **Dubai:** **most pro-STR regulator globally** — DET holiday-home permit, owners can self-register up to ~8 units, Tourism Dirham AED 10–15/night passed to guests. Friction = building/developer NOC (can disqualify a specific unit) and 3-hour guest-data upload. Tail risk low [T1].

**Management options & economics:**
- *Independent local manager:* lowest fee, highest variance, key-person risk.
- *Platform-managed / full-service:* 15–35% of revenue depending on market (Australia 15–25%, Asia 20–35%); the **advertised-vs-realised gap** is real — assume the top of the range.
- *Hotel-flag conversion (Marriott Homes & Villas, Hyatt, Hilton Curio) / branded residence (Aman, Six Senses):* higher fees but solves the legality problem in Phuket (hotel licence), the management problem (light oversight — the investor's stated mandate), and the marketing/occupancy problem. **For a light-oversight, annual-visit investor, the branded/hotel-licensed model is the natural fit and should be the default screen, not the exception.**

**Platform concentration:** Airbnb dependence is real; diversify across Booking.com/Agoda (Asia) and Stayz (Australia). A market whose regulator targets "Airbnb-style" listings specifically (Bali) raises this risk.

**Insurance & climate (priced into the cost stack):** Bali volcanic/tsunami/flood with thin, expensive cover and no title insurance; Phuket monsoon/tsunami, cheap base insurance but flood riders extra; Gold Coast cyclone (TC Alfred 2025)/flood/coastal erosion with **escalating, partly-uninsurable** premiums; Dubai post-April-2024 flood risk (+15% premiums, multi-year drainage remediation). Underwrite insurance as a *rising* cost line, not a fixed one.

## Dimension 7 — Macro & Geopolitical

**The screen:** *FX drift vs INR, tourism demand durability and source-market concentration, sovereign/geopolitical stability, 20-year climate risk.*

**FX vs INR (10-year structural drift — the engine-2 multiplier):** INR depreciates ~3.4%/yr vs USD structurally [T2 citing RBI].

| Pair | INR drift p.a. | Reliability of the tailwind |
|---|---|---|
| **AED/INR** | ~−3.4% (INR weakens) | **Cleanest** — AED pegged 3.6725/USD, tracks USD exactly [T1] |
| **AUD/INR** | ~−2 to −3% | Real but **volatile** (51→69 within a 52-week window) |
| **THB/INR** | ~−1.5 to −2% | Mild, stable |
| **IDR/INR** | ~0 to slightly negative | **Least reliable** — IDR also depreciates vs USD, can erode the INR benefit |

The investor implication is sharp: **the Dubai FX tailwind (~3.4%/yr) is the single cleanest FX story — but it is identical to the USD/INR drift obtainable far more cheaply and liquidly via US index funds.** It is therefore *not, by itself, a reason to buy Dubai property*; it must be earned on the property's own operating merits.

**Tourism source-market concentration:** **India is a top-3 source for all four markets and #1 for Dubai** — strong alignment with the investor's home-market read, but a concentration risk. **China is the swing factor** (upside for Bali/Phuket/Gold Coast if it recovers, current drag — Thailand 2025 arrivals fell on China −33.5%). **Russia** is the fragile pillar under Phuket and Dubai.

**Sovereign/geopolitical & climate (20-yr):** Australia (stable sovereign, AAA, but coastal/climate insurance escalation) · UAE (stable, USD-pegged, regional security + flood/heat/water) · Thailand (political cycle, Russian dependency) · Indonesia (current-account deficit, IDR weakness, land-reform trajectory, volcanic/seismic). On a 20-year horizon, **Gold Coast and Dubai carry the lower sovereign/structural risk; Bali the highest.**

## Dimension 8 — Sizing & Cross-Market Correlation

**The screen:** *Do these markets diversify, and what is the minimum viable cheque per market below which STR economics break?*

**Correlation (qualitative, property / tourism):** property cycles diversify *moderately* because the credit regimes differ — **Gold Coast is the only true mortgage/rate-driven market (RBA), Bali/Phuket/Dubai are cash-buyer/supply-driven.** Tourism cycles are **more correlated** (global travel is a common factor) but offset by differing source-market mix.

| | Bali | Phuket | Gold Coast | Dubai |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bali** | — | **High** | Low–Med | Low |
| **Phuket** | High | — | Low | Low–Med |
| **Gold Coast** | Low–Med | Low | — | **Low (best diversifier pair)** |
| **Dubai** | Low | Low–Med | Low | — |

**Bali–Phuket is the most correlated pair** (shared APAC oversupply + overlapping Australian/Chinese/Indian source markets) — **holding both is concentration, not diversification.** **Dubai is the best diversifier** (USD-pegged FX, India-led demand, oil-liquidity cycle). The binding common risk that overrides all diversification is a **synchronised global liquidity downturn** (the 18-year-cycle bust) hitting travel demand and asset prices together.

**Minimum viable cheque (below which fixed management cost breaks the economics):**
- Bali: a sub-USD-200k studio cannot carry full-service villa management + PT PMA compliance; **target USD 350k+ for a managed villa.**
- Phuket: a hotel-licensed branded condo realistically **USD 250k+**.
- Gold Coast: beachfront strata fixed costs argue for **USD 450k+ (≈A$700k+)**.
- Dubai: service-charge drag argues for **USD 350k+** in a quality building.

## Dimension 9 — Pre-Committed Pillar-Break Criteria

**The screen (the property analogue to an equity thesis-break):** *Specify, before deploying, the criteria that force an exit — so the decision is made when you are rational, not when the asset is falling.*

**Asset-level (any property, any market):**
- Occupancy below the underwriting floor for **2 consecutive years** (Bali/Phuket <50%, Dubai <55%, Gold Coast <40% citywide STR baseline).
- **Net post-tax INR yield falls below 3%** for 2 consecutive years.
- Cumulative refurbishment/capex exceeds **20% of asset value** inside any 5-year window.

**Market-level:**
- STR **ban or material restriction** enacted (Phuket 30-day enforcement hardens building-wide; Bali blanket ban; Gold Coast adopts NSW-style 180-day cap).
- **Foreign-ownership tightening** (Thailand quota cut, Bali PT PMA reform, Australia FIRB exemption narrowed).
- Supply pipeline exceeds **~24 months of absorption** in the sub-market.

**Macro-level:**
- Source-currency depreciates **>15% vs USD, sustained over 18 months** (Bali IDR is the live risk here).
- Political regime change with property-rights implications; capital controls imposed.

**Indian-side / life-stage:**
- Tax regime change that makes the effective Indian leakage prohibitive (e.g., FTC tightening, surcharge restructuring, removal of the LTCG surcharge cap).
- **Spouse residency status changes** → loss of the Gold Coast FIRB-exemption pathway.
- FEMA tightening on foreign-property repatriation.

Write these into the purchase decision **before** capital moves; review annually against the actuals.

## Dimension 10 — Twin-Engine Arithmetic (10-year scenarios, INR after-tax)

**The screen:** *Decompose return into Engine 1 (net rental yield, reinvested) × Engine 2 (capital appreciation in INR = local appreciation × FX), run bear/base/bull, and benchmark against a 12–15% INR equity return.*

**Method & assumptions (stated explicitly, illustrative not predictive):** 10-year hold; Engine 1 = net post-tax INR yield (Dimension 2) compounded; Engine 2 = local-currency price CAGR + the market's INR/FX drift (Dimension 7); exit taxed per Dimension 5f; recommended *structures* modelled (PT-PMA-HGB for Bali, hotel-licensed condo-freehold for Phuket, joint-tenant freehold for Gold Coast, freehold for Dubai). **Leasehold variants of Bali/Phuket are materially worse and are not the modelled case.** IRRs are approximate and rounded.

| Market | Scenario | Engine 1 (post-tax yield) | Engine 2 (local + FX) | Exit-tax drag | **≈ 10-yr IRR (INR, after-tax)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bali** | Bear | ~2.8% | −4% local, ~−1% FX | 2.5% of price | **negative to ~2%** |
| (PT PMA villa) | Base | ~4.2% | +2% local, ~0% FX | low | **~5.5%** |
| | Bull | ~5.6% | +6% local, ~0% FX | low | **~10%** |
| **Phuket** | Bear | ~2.5% | −3% local, +1.75% FX | progressive | **~1–2%** |
| (licensed condo) | Base | ~3.2% | +2% local, +1.75% FX | ~1% | **~6%** |
| | Bull | ~3.9% | +6% local, +1.75% FX | ~1% | **~10–11%** |
| **Gold Coast** | Bear | ~2.0% | −2% local, +2.5% FX | ~30%+ on gain | **~2–3%** |
| (freehold) | Base | ~2.0% | +4% local, +2.5% FX | **heavy (no discount + 15% FRCGW)** | **~6–7%** |
| | Bull | ~2.5% | +7% local, +2.5% FX | heavy | **~9–10%** |
| **Dubai** | Bear | ~2.8% | −3% local (or −15% yr-1), +3.4% FX | 12.5% | **~3–4% (lower if correction hits at entry)** |
| (freehold) | Base | ~3.9% | +3% local, +3.4% FX | 12.5% | **~9%** |
| | Bull | ~4.6% | +7% local, +3.4% FX | 12.5% | **~13–14%** |

**The decisive conclusion:** **in every base case, the after-tax INR IRR (≈6–9%) sits below the investor's own 12–15% equity hurdle.** Only the **bull cases** approach or meet it, and only **Dubai's bull case (~13–14%)** comfortably clears — driven by the clean ~3.4% FX tailwind plus the low 12.5% exit tax and the lightest management. Total tax leakage runs **25–35% of gross return across all four**, with Gold Coast's exit-tax regime the heaviest drag and Dubai's the lightest.

This is the honest answer the framework was built to surface: **international STR is not a yield-arbitrage that beats Indian equity on a base-case after-tax basis.** Its case rests on (a) the bull scenario, (b) the FX tailwind, (c) genuine diversification away from a late-cycle, expensive Indian smid-cap market, and (d) lifestyle/visa optionality — **and on buying after the forecast 2026 cycle peak, not into it.** Each of those four must be argued explicitly for any deployment to clear the bar; none can be assumed.

## Reusing this template for any future RE decision

Run the 10 screens in this order; stop at the first hard fail:
1. **Legal/Structural** — is the interest durable, transferable, enforceable? (leasehold/quota/structure-fragility = caution)
2. **Tax stack** — all-in leakage as % of rent and % of gain, Indian-resident lens, FTC failure modes.
3. **Net post-tax INR yield** — the only yield that exists.
4. **STR legality & operating model** — is the rental model legal; is management light enough for the mandate?
5. **Cycle position** — are you buying a peak?
6. **Appreciation drivers** — structural vs cyclical; FX-stripped.
7. **Macro/FX** — INR drift reliability, source-market concentration.
8. **Correlation/sizing** — does it diversify; is the cheque above minimum viable?
9. **Pre-committed exit criteria** — written before deployment.
10. **Twin-engine scenarios** — does the base case beat the liquid alternative after all friction?

A property must clear **all ten**. The matrix (Deliverable 3) scores the four current markets against this template; the checklist (Deliverable 4) is the one-page pre-buy gate.
