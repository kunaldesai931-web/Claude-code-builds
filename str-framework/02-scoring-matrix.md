# Deliverable 3 — Comparative Market Scoring Matrix

**All four markets scored across all 10 framework dimensions, weighted for a yield-led mandate, with an explicit ranking and a recommended 2–3-property allocation.**

> Scoring: **1 = kills the thesis · 5 = best-in-class.** Scores are the analyst's synthesis of the underlying research (see the framework and the bear-case docs for the evidence and source tiers behind each). Weights reflect the investor's stated priority ranking — **yield dominant, then appreciation, then residency/visa optionality, then lifestyle** — combined with the two dimensions that most often *kill* a deal for an Indian resident (Tax and Legal). The weights sum to 100%.

## Recommended 2–3-property allocation (USD 750k–1.5M, yield-led, 15–25-yr hold, light oversight)

The allocation is governed by three findings from the framework: **(i)** base-case after-tax IRRs sit below the 12–15% equity hurdle, so deployment must be *disciplined and phased*, not eager; **(ii)** all four markets are at a forecast cycle peak (~2026), so the largest tranche should be reserved for the cycle low; **(iii)** structure quality within a market matters more than the choice of city.

**Recommended structure — phase across the 3–5 year window:**

| Tranche | Timing | Market & structure | Approx. cheque | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Property 1 — core (yield + ops)** | Deploy on a **price correction / post-2026-supply-wave entry**, not at today's peak | **Dubai freehold**, quality building outside the 2026–27 supply-spike zones (avoid JVC/Business Bay for entry), DET-licensed holiday home, full-service managed | **USD 500–650k** | Highest net yield + lightest management (fits the light-oversight mandate) + cleanest FX + India #1 demand + low 12.5% exit tax |
| **Property 2 — appreciation + optionality** | Phase in; FIRB joint-tenant structure confirmed in writing first | **Gold Coast freehold**, joint tenants with the citizen spouse, Burleigh/Broadbeach (tight vacancy), QLD strata cannot ban STR | **USD 450–550k (≈A$700–850k)** | Title/legal certainty, structural appreciation, AUD/INR tailwind, residency optionality via the spouse pathway. *Plan the exit around the non-resident CGT/FRCGW regime — hold long, take written tax advice on transferring economic interest to the resident-of-Australia spouse over time* |
| **Property 3 — optional, yield top-up** | **Only as a cycle-low add**, not a peak deployment | **Phuket hotel-licensed condo-freehold** (legal daily STR, branded/managed) *or* a **Bali PT-PMA-HGB villa** with full KBLI 55193 licensing | **USD 300–400k** | Yield maximisation per the mandate — but carries the higher-risk flags (Phuket: Russian-demand + oversupply; Bali: tenure + enforcement). **Default recommendation: hold this tranche as dry powder and deploy into the cycle low, or skip it and add to Indian large-cap, rather than buy a third late-cycle asset at the peak** |

**Net recommendation:** a **two-property core (Dubai + Gold Coast)** captures the best risk-adjusted combination — Dubai for yield/ops/FX, Gold Coast for title certainty/appreciation/optionality — and they are the **best diversifier pair** of the four. They are *deliberately* the two markets the yield-led screen does *not* rank highest on headline yield, because once the Indian-resident tax overlay and ownership-durability screens are applied, **Bali and Phuket's higher headline yields do not survive.** Treat the third property as a *cycle-timed option*, not a commitment, and avoid holding the correlated Bali+Phuket pair together.

**Hard caveats before any of this is actionable:**
- Confirm the **FIRB joint-tenant exemption extends to an investment/STR purpose** in writing with a QLD property lawyer (grey area, single-/low-sourced) before any Gold Coast exchange.
- Underwrite Dubai at **conservative occupancy (mid-40s to 60%, not 73%+)** and **after the full service-charge/chiller/DET cost stack** — verify against paid AirDNA MarketMinder and DLD transaction data.
- Treat all T3 yield figures as upper-bound marketing; re-derive net post-tax INR yield from primary cost and tax data before committing capital.
