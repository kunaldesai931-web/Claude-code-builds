# Deliverable 1 — The Bear Case (read this first)

> *Mandatory before any conviction summary. The question this section answers is not "is there a deal here?" but: **what would have to be true for international STR exposure by an Indian tax resident to be the worst capital-allocation decision of the next 15 years — and how much of that is already true today?***

> Source-tier convention used throughout this framework: **T1** = regulatory/primary (tax authority, central bank, statute, treaty text); **T2** = institutional/professional (law firm, Big-4, established data vendor, quality media); **T3** = property-marketing / broker / aggregator blog (directional only, never treated as analytically equal to T1/T2). `STALE` = data older than 12 months at the date of writing (May 2026). Single-sourced claims are flagged inline.

---

## The structural bear case (applies to all four markets at once)

International STR for an Indian tax resident is the worst decision of the next 15 years if the following chain holds — and note that **most links in it are not forecasts, they are current facts**:

1. **You are buying the whole basket at the top of the 18-year land cycle.** Akhil Patel / Phillip Anderson / Fred Harrison (the Hoyt land-cycle lineage) place the global peak **around 2026**, followed by a **~4-year downturn into roughly 2030** [T2 — MoneyWeek 9-Jul-2021; *The Secret Wealth Advantage*, Harriman House 2023; the "2028 trough" is model-implied, not a dated quote]. A 3–5 year deployment window that front-loads capital in 2026 is, on this framework, buying into the single highest-risk entry window of the cycle. The Howard Marks pendulum reads **late-cycle in all four markets**, euphoric in Dubai and oversupplied in Bali.

2. **The Indian-resident tax overlay quietly removes 25–35% of the headline before you start.** Net rental income is taxed in India at slab (≈31% with cess, more once surcharge engages); foreign LTCG is 12.5% with **no indexation and — critically for a resident — no foreign-currency protection** (the s.48 forex proviso is NRI-only, so rupee depreciation is taxed as if it were real gain) [T1 — CBDT Budget 2024 FAQ, PIB PRID 2036604; s.48 provisos]. Every "9–12% net yield" pitch in this asset class is quoted *before* this layer.

3. **The friction you cannot see on a brochure compounds.** LRS caps you at **USD 250k per person per financial year** [T1 — RBI LRS Master Direction], so a USD 750k–1.5M programme is a multi-year staging exercise. Each above-₹10L remittance carries **20% TCS** locked up for 12–18 months until refunded [T1 — Finance Act 2025, s.206C(1G)]. FX spreads (0.5–2% per leg), wire fees and bank compliance delays recur on every cycle of capital. None of this appears in an IRR brochure.

4. **The lumpy exit detonates the surcharge cliff.** Your base income sits just below ₹50L. A single property sale realising a large LTCG drags **total** income across the ₹50L (and possibly ₹1Cr) threshold, and while surcharge on the gain slice is capped at 15%, the surcharge on your *ordinary* income is **not** — the gain retroactively surcharges your salary and rent [T1 — Finance Act 2022 surcharge cap; worked example in the framework doc]. A "tax-efficient" exit is a fiction unless realisations are staggered across financial years.

5. **The disclosure tail is asymmetric and unforgiving.** Foreign immovable property must be reported in **Schedule FA every year regardless of value** (the ₹20L safe-harbour explicitly excludes immovable property), and a missed disclosure carries a **₹10 lakh penalty per year under the Black Money Act 2015 — even if every rupee of tax was paid** [T1 — BMA 2015 s.42/43; T2 — Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan]. CRS/AEOI data-sharing (UAE, Australia, Thailand, Indonesia all participate) makes non-disclosure a notice waiting to happen. This is the highest-severity, lowest-awareness trap in the entire programme.

6. **The benchmark you are escaping is not cheap either — but it is liquid.** Indian mid/small-cap is 25–50%+ above long-run P/E (SEBI/AMFI issued froth warnings, Mar-2025) [T1/T2], so the equity opportunity cost is real. But Indian large-cap at ~22.5x is roughly fair, and — decisively — **a Nifty position can be exited in a day with a 10% LTCG and no Schedule FA, no FRCGW withholding, no probate, no FX round-trip.** International STR is the illiquid, friction-heavy, disclosure-heavy alternative to a liquid asset. The bear case is simply that you are taking on all of that illiquidity and friction for a base-case after-tax INR IRR that (per the twin-engine arithmetic) clusters at **6–9% — below your own 12–15% equity hurdle in every base case, and only competitive in the bull cases.**

**If all six hold, the verdict is unambiguous: do not deploy, or deploy only the minimum needed to buy optionality, and wait for the cycle low.**

## Market-by-market bear case

### Bali — "you never owned it, and then it was illegal"
- **No freehold for foreigners.** The only sound structure is a **PT PMA holding HGB** (an 80-year right, 30+20+30); leasehold (Hak Sewa, 25–30 yrs) **reverts to the landowner at expiry** with renewal renegotiated at then-market — a terminal-value cliff that turns a "yielding asset" into a **wasting asset** over a 15–25 year hold [T3 advisory, multi-sourced; verify against BKPM/BPN].
- **Nominee structures are now criminalised.** Bali Perda 4/2026 reportedly imposes up to 5 years' prison + IDR 1bn fine on nominee transfers [T2/T3, single-sourced — verify]. The cheap shortcut everyone uses is now a felony.
- **Foreigners cannot hold a Pondok Wisata licence**; compliant STR requires PT PMA + KBLI 55193 villa licence + the **March-2026 NIB/licensing deadline** for OTA-listed property, enforced by demolitions and deportations (48 structures razed at Bingin Beach, Jul-2025) [T2/T3].
- **Canggu is already oversupplied** — villa inventory +35% in three years, >40% listing growth against falling occupancy [T3, single-sourced but directionally credible]. You are buying late-cycle supply.
- **Tax-on-gross + FX bleed:** 20% gross rental WHT and **2.5% final tax on the gross sale price** (not the gain), stacked on structural IDR weakness (record ~17,800/USD). The Bali FX tailwind to INR is the **least reliable of the four** — IDR depreciates against USD too.
- **Tail catastrophe, thin cover:** active Agung volcano, recurring 2025 floods, tsunami/seismic exposure, no title insurance.

### Phuket — "the rental model is illegal and the lease just got shorter"
- **Daily STR is illegal without a hotel licence.** The Hotel Act caps you at **30-day minimum stays** unless you buy into a purpose-built, hotel-licensed condo. A single juristic-person rule change or complaint can force you to 30-day minimums overnight and gut the economics [T2].
- **Leasehold lost its premise in 2025.** Supreme Court Case 4655/2566 (18-Mar-2025) ruled lease terms beyond 30 years void and **pre-agreed "30+30+30" auto-renewals unenforceable** [T1 via T2]. The marketing premise behind nearly every "90-year" Phuket villa lease is now legally dead. Thai-company nominee workarounds are being prosecuted with AI-driven detection (820+ cases) [T2].
- **Condo freehold quota is a liquidity trap.** The 49% foreign quota is enforced at registration; in a foreigner-heavy building, **resale to another foreigner requires quota headroom at that future date** — a constraint you don't control.
- **Mid-market oversupply:** 10,000+ units into 2026 atop ~7,000 unsold in Bang Tao/Surin, with developer **guaranteed-yield schemes** (classic late-cycle marketing — the yield is priced into an inflated purchase price) [T1 C9 Hotelworks; T2 REIC/Knight Frank].
- **Demand concentration in fragile Russian money** (~40% of foreign buyers) — a sanctions/FX/geopolitical reversal removes the marginal buyer and the marginal guest simultaneously.

### Gold Coast — "the cleanest title, the cruelest exit tax"
- **The non-resident CGT regime is the dealbreaker.** A foreign resident gets **no 50% CGT discount** (gains since 2012 fully taxed at ≥30%), and from **1-Jan-2025 a 15% Foreign Resident CGT Withholding is taken from the gross sale price** at settlement, trapping capital until an Australian return reconciles it [T1 — ATO]. This roughly doubles effective CGT versus a resident and savages exit IRR.
- **Rental taxed at 30% from the first dollar** (no tax-free threshold for foreign residents), plus QLD **8% AFAD** at purchase and a **2% foreign land-tax surcharge** annually — a triple foreign-investor load [T1 — ATO, QRO].
- **The FIRB edge is structurally fragile.** The spouse exemption survives **only as joint tenants** with the citizen spouse; any tenants-in-common / asset-protection structuring breaks it, and the **established-dwelling ban (now extended to 30-Jun-2029)** then bars the foreign spouse from buying established stock at all [T1 — Treasury/ATO; T2 — HSF Kramer]. Single load-bearing point: get written conveyancing advice before exchange. Whether the exemption even extends to a pure *investment/STR* purpose is a grey area [T2/T3, uncertain].
- **Lowest yield of the four** (gross unit yields ~4–5%) against high beachfront strata (often A$8k–20k+/yr), escalating and partly-uninsurable climate premiums (TC Alfred 2025 pushed cyclone risk south; coastal/sea-level inundation generally uninsured), into a market where the **RBA reversed its 2025 cuts back to 4.35% in 2026** and affordability is stretched at ~A$1.17m house medians.

### Dubai — "tax-free for everyone except you, into the teeth of the supply wave"
- **The tax-free illusion is decisive.** UAE levies zero tax on rent and gains — so for an Indian resident there is **no foreign tax to credit**, and you pay the **full Indian tax** (≈31% on net rent, 12.5% LTCG) [T2 — confirmed by ITAT *Shah Rukh Khan* line of reasoning; Rule 128]. The headline "0% Dubai tax" delivers **no benefit** to you; it simply moves the tax to India. Every Dubai pitch aimed at Indians is built on this illusion.
- **Oversupply into a late cycle.** Prices are up ~78% from the 2020 trough; the **2025–2028 pipeline is ~200,000–300,000 units with a 2027 spike**, concentrated in the STR-heavy areas (JVC, Business Bay), and **Fitch has flagged up to a ~15% correction** [T2 — DLD via media; Fitch]. Off-plan is ~63% of sales and priced *above* ready stock — a classic euphoria signature.
- **The STR yield premium evaporates after the cost stack** — 15–25% management + high service charges (AED 10–30+/sqft) + chiller/DEWA + DET fees + summer voids drag the advertised "8–12%" toward ordinary long-let yields (~5–6%). Reported occupancy diverges wildly by source (41.6% vs 73%) — a data-quality red flag; do not underwrite at the high end.
- **Fragile hot-money demand + boom-bust DNA:** heavy post-2022 Russian buyer dependence (~11% of foreign transactions), atop a market that fell ~50% in 2008–09. Sentiment-driven and reversible.
- **Physical tail:** the April-2024 floods were the heaviest rain in 75 years (~US$3bn insured losses, +15% property premiums); drainage remediation is a multi-year, AED 30bn programme.

## What the bear case does *not* say

It does not say the asset class is uninvestable. It says:
- The **base case loses to your own equity hurdle**, so the case must rest on bull scenarios, the FX tailwind, diversification, and lifestyle/visa optionality — and those must be argued explicitly, not assumed.
- **Cycle timing dominates.** Deploying the full envelope into a forecast 2026 peak is the error the framework is built to prevent. Phasing across the 3–5 year window to straddle the downturn — and keeping dry powder for the cycle low — is the single highest-value decision.
- **Structure quality dominates within a market.** Freehold/PT-PMA-HGB beats leasehold; a hotel-licensed Phuket condo beats an illegal daily-let; joint-tenant FIRB-exempt Gold Coast beats a broken structure. Most of the loss in this asset class comes from buying the wrong *structure*, not the wrong *city*.

Only after internalising the above should you read the conviction-side material in the framework, matrix, and checklist documents.
